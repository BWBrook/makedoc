# Figure captions (v2)

**Figure 1.** Insurance simulations under mixed versus aligned responses. Community variability and realised growth synchrony for mixed response slopes (directionally diverse) versus positive response slopes (aligned). **(A)** Heatmap of $\mathrm{CV}_{\mathrm{tot}}$ across innovation SD $\sigma_{\mathrm{env}}$ (columns) and imposed environmental correlation $\rho_{\mathrm{env}}$ (rows) for mixed slopes with $S=4$. **(B)** As in panel A but for positive slopes with $S=4$. A shared colour scale is used across A-B to highlight magnitude contrast. **(C)** $\mathrm{CV}_{\mathrm{tot}}$ versus imposed correlation (plotted on $\rho_{\mathrm{eff}}$) at $\sigma_{\mathrm{env}}=0.5$ for $S=4$ and $S=8$; points show replicate values and lines show scenario means. **(D)** Realised growth synchrony (mean pairwise correlation of per-capita growth rates) versus imposed driver correlation. The dashed line indicates equality ($y=x$). Mixed slopes show strong decoupling of imposed and realised synchrony, while positive slopes track imposed correlation much more closely.

**Figure 2.** Richness effects on community variability under mixed response directions. Scenario means of $\mathrm{CV}_{\mathrm{tot}}$ as a function of species richness $S$ for mixed-slope communities, averaged over the shared non-negative imposed-correlation subset ($\rho_{\mathrm{eff}}\ge 0$) for each $\sigma_{\mathrm{env}}$. Over the examined richness range ($S=2$-8), $\mathrm{CV}_{\mathrm{tot}}$ declines strongly for all $\sigma_{\mathrm{env}}>0$, while the $\sigma_{\mathrm{env}}=0$ baseline remains near zero.
